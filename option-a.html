How venom immunotherapy helps

After testing confirms which insects you react to, a course of venom shots retrains your immune system and can greatly lower the chance of a severe reaction from a future sting. TRAAC offers rapid venom protocols to get you protected sooner.

Questions

Food allergy treatment FAQ

Does OIT cure a food allergy?

Usually not completely. OIT raises the amount it takes to cause a reaction. Some patients reach "free eating," and a few may achieve lasting tolerance.

Do we still carry epinephrine?

Yes. Keep carrying epinephrine as your provider directs.

Food SLIT or OIT?

Food SLIT uses tiny amounts under the tongue with only four scheduled office visits and fewer precautions. OIT can reach full servings but needs more precautions.

How young can treatment start?

FirstStep OIT™ and Rapid Pre-Ingestion OIT™ are designed for babies and toddlers.

Are there precautions during Full OIT?

Yes, such as avoiding exercise and heat around dosing, following illness-related instructions, and dosing regularly as prescribed.

Allergy drops: treat the cause, needle-free

How it works

Small doses. Steady change.

Your immune system treats harmless pollen or dust like a threat. Daily low-dose exposure under the tongue helps it learn to tolerate those allergens. Drag the slider to walk through treatment.

Who is a good fit?

Drops can be a practical choice when a few allergens drive most symptoms. Shots may be better when many allergens need treatment.

Does it work?

Studies show SLIT can reduce symptoms for certain environmental allergies. The strongest evidence is for specific allergens and doses studied in trials; the benefit of custom-mixed drops is less certain, and results vary. We track your symptoms and medication use to measure your response.

Questions

Allergy drops FAQ

About FDA status. Custom allergy drops are prescribed off label in the U.S. The liquid drops are not FDA approved for use under the tongue. Certain SLIT tablets are FDA approved for specific allergens.
Do I need to come into the office?

Not necessarily. You can be seen at our Southlake or Fort Worth office, or use our Allergy Drops by Mail program: telemedicine visits, labs near you, and drops shipped to your door.

What are the side effects?

Mouth or tongue itching is common early on. Serious allergic reactions are possible. Follow your dosing and safety instructions, tell us about worsening asthma, and seek emergency care for trouble breathing, throat swelling or fainting.

When will I feel better?

Improvement is gradual and may take several months. Keep taking your usual allergy medicines until your provider tells you to change them.

How long is treatment?

Treatment generally continues for several years. Your provider reviews your response and decides with you when to stop.

Can children use allergy drops?

Drops can be an option for kids. Your child's provider will decide what's right after an evaluation.

Patient guide

Allergy drops: what to know

Answers from TRAAC's patient guide to sublingual immunotherapy.

Open the patient guide (PDF)
Are drops FDA approved? Custom allergy drops are prescribed off label in the U.S. The liquid drops are not FDA approved for use under the tongue; certain SLIT tablets are FDA approved for specific allergens.
How does the process work?

We review your symptoms and allergy testing to select the allergens most relevant to you. If drops are a good fit, we prepare your treatment and give you instructions for a gradual build-up to a daily maintenance dose. You take the drops under your tongue at home and have periodic follow-up visits.

Who is a good candidate?

Drops may be a practical option when a few allergens are the main drivers of symptoms. Allergy shots may be a better fit when many allergens need treatment. We also review asthma control and your medical history before recommending immunotherapy.

Does it work?

Studies show sublingual immunotherapy can reduce symptoms for certain environmental allergies. The strongest evidence is for specific allergens and doses studied in clinical trials. The benefit of custom-mixed drops is less certain, and individual results vary. We track your symptoms and medication use to assess your response.

What are the risks?

Mouth or tongue itching and irritation may occur, particularly early in treatment. Serious allergic reactions are possible. Follow your dosing and safety instructions, and tell us about worsening asthma or concerning symptoms. Seek emergency care for breathing difficulty, throat swelling, faintness or other signs of a severe reaction.

When should I expect improvement?

Improvement is gradual and may take several months. We assess progress over time, including during your usual allergy seasons. Continue your prescribed allergy medications until we advise you to change them.

How long is treatment?

Treatment generally continues for several years. Our team reviews your response with you and decides when it's appropriate to stop.

What will it cost?

Most insurance plans do not cover custom allergy drops. Cost depends on your formula and treatment plan. Call (817) 421-0770, ext. 142 for a personal quote.

Frequently asked questions

Good questions, clear answers

What conditions does TRAAC treat?

A wide range of allergy, asthma and immune conditions, including seasonal and environmental allergies, food allergies, asthma, hives and eczema, sinus problems, insect sting allergies and immune system disorders.

How does TRAAC test for allergies?

We use skin-prick testing, blood tests and, when needed, medically supervised challenge tests to identify specific allergens. See all our tests.

Do you treat children and adults?

Yes. We care for babies, children, teens and adults. Treatment is personalized to each patient's age, allergies, medical history and needs.

What treatment options do you offer?

Medications and avoidance plans, allergy shots (including rapid rush protocols), allergy drops, food allergy desensitization (OIT and food SLIT), biologic therapies such as Xolair, immunoglobulin replacement and venom immunotherapy. See everything we do.

How do I know which immunotherapy is right for me?

It depends on your allergies, symptoms, medical history, lifestyle and goals. Your provider will recommend the immunotherapy or desensitization option that fits you best.

How long does treatment take?

It varies by the type of immunotherapy, your allergy and how you respond. Your team will walk you through the expected timeline.

What are the possible side effects?

Most side effects are mild and temporary, but more serious reactions can occur. Your provider will review risks, benefits and safety steps with you before you start.
